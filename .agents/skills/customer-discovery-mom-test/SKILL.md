# Customer Discovery (Mom Test)

## Goal
Learn what real people do and pay for, not what they politely say about my idea.

## Lenses
- Rob Fitzpatrick, The Mom Test: ask about their life and past behavior, never about whether they like my idea. Avoid compliments, hypotheticals, and pitching.
- Sean Ellis product-market-fit survey: ask "How would you feel if you could no longer use this product?" with answers Very disappointed / Somewhat disappointed / Not disappointed. A commonly cited signal is around 40 percent "very disappointed". Treat that as a rough guide, not a law.
- Rahul Vohra and Superhuman: focus on the people who love the product most, learn why, and fix what holds back the "somewhat" group.
- Jobs-to-be-done: what job is the user hiring Vid-Visual for?

## Deliverables I may ask for
1. Interview script: 10 questions about the last time they made a video or visual, what tools they used, what was painful, what it cost in time and money, and what they tried to fix it.
2. Recruiting messages for X, Reddit communities, WhatsApp or Telegram groups, and college groups. Read each community's rules first. No spam, no mass copy-paste.
3. In-app micro-survey spec: when it shows (after the 2nd export, never before the first success), what it asks, where answers are stored.
4. Feedback synthesis: tag each comment by theme, count frequency, keep verbatim quotes, and separate pain (a problem they have) from request (a solution they suggest). Pain is data. Requests are hypotheses.
5. Decision output: top 3 insights, what to build, what to stop, and the next test.

## Rules
- Never lead the witness. Never pitch during an interview.
- Ask for commitment (time, money, an introduction), not praise.
- Say how small the sample is. 5 interviews give direction, not proof.
- Get consent before recording. Store the minimum personal data.
- Label every conclusion as "observed" or "hypothesis".
