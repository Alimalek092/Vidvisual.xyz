# Positioning and Messaging

## Goal
Make it obvious in 5 seconds who Vid-Visual is for, what it does, and why it beats the alternatives.

## Lens
April Dunford, Obviously Awesome: positioning has five parts. Competitive alternatives, unique attributes, the value those attributes give, the customers who care most, and the market category that makes the value easy to understand. Apply the framework, do not quote the book.

## Process
1. Alternatives: list what people use today instead, including doing nothing, manual editing, templates tools, stock footage, and other AI generators. Use web search to find the real current alternatives. Cite sources. Never invent competitors or features.
2. Unique attributes: what Vid-Visual can do that those alternatives cannot, or do worse. Read the actual product and repo to confirm each one.
3. Value: translate each attribute into a result the user cares about (time saved, cost saved, quality gained).
4. Best-fit customers: who gets the most value and feels the problem hardest. Be specific (for example "solo creators who post weekly") instead of "everyone".
5. Category: the frame that makes the above easy to grasp.

## Output
- A positioning canvas with the five parts above.
- One sentence: "For [who] who [job], Vid-Visual is [category] that [main benefit], unlike [alternative] which [limit]."
- 3 headline options with a one-line reason for each.
- A proof list: what evidence we have and what we still need (demo outputs, numbers, testimonials from real users).
- Top 5 objections and a short honest answer for each.

## Rules
- Concrete beats clever. Ban: revolutionary, next-gen, game-changing, unleash, seamless, cutting-edge.
- Every claim must be demonstrable in the product. Mark any claim that needs proof.
- Test the headline: could a stranger repeat it back after 5 seconds?
